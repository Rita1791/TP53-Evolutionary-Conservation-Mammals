# Result Interpretation for Non-Specialists

## Purpose

This file explains the main result of the TP53 mammalian conservation project in accessible language for readers who may not be specialists in comparative genomics, cancer biology, or computational biology.

---

## What Is TP53?

TP53 is a gene that encodes the p53 protein. The p53 protein helps protect cells from becoming cancerous by responding to DNA damage, controlling cell division, and promoting cell death when damage is severe.

Because of this important role, TP53 is often called a tumour-suppressor gene.

---

## What Are Mutation Hotspots?

In many cancers, mutations in TP53 do not occur randomly across the whole protein. Some positions are mutated again and again in different tumours and different patients.

These frequently mutated positions are called mutation hotspots.
